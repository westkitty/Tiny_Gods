# Visual Comparative Recon — Phase III

STUDIED GAMES (bounded recon, 8 sources):

1. WORLD BOX
Mechanism studied: civilization legibility at tiny scale, readable settlements, visible destruction.
Works: tiny settlements with clear shape; destruction is obvious; zoomed-out readability.
Does NOT transfer: its mobile UI style; bright cartoon palette doesn't fit Tiny Gods myth.
Adapted principle: settlements must have silhouette at any zoom; destruction must leave visible scars.
Avoid: copying direct pixel art style or color palette.

2. REUS 2
Mechanism studied: biome silhouette, terrain shape language, palette separation, ecosystem readability.
Works: each biome immediately readable from shape; color separation is clean.
Adapted principle: terrain must have distinctive silhouette (plains open, forest clustered, river continuous, mountain angular).
Avoid: copying exact texture patterns.

3. DWARF FORTRESS (graphical edition)
Mechanism studied: readable sprites at tiny scale, iconography, personality at small size, information density.
Works: sprites remain recognizable at very small resolution; culture and occupation readable.
Adapted principle: creature sprites must have distinct shapes and accessories at gameplay scale; occupation must be visible without hovering.
Avoid: copying ASCII/text representation.

4. SONGS OF SYX
Mechanism studied: visualizing large populations, readable city growth, scale transitions.
Works: settlement growth is visually progressive; buildings add density without chaos.
Adapted principle: settlement buildings must show progressive clustering and density change.

5. AGAINST THE STORM
Mechanism studied: atmosphere, weather, environmental storytelling, lighting, settlement readability, strong silhouettes.
Works: weather transforms the visual mood; settlements have clear identity; lighting creates depth.
Adapted principle: weather (rain, wind, fire effects) must visibly alter the scene; lighting must separate terrain layers.

6. THE UNIVERSIM
Mechanism studied: spectacle of god powers, large-scale environmental feedback, making intervention feel physical.
Works: powers change terrain visibly; large-scale effects have anticipation and aftermath.
Adapted principle: divine interventions must have anticipation (build-up), impact, and aftermath (scars/recovery), not instant single-frame effects.

7. BLACK & WHITE (reference only)
Mechanism: divine presence as physical world change; atmosphere; mythic scale.
Adapted: silence as a meaningful absence; divine events must alter geography, not just apply temporary overlays.

8. FROM DUST
Mechanism: environmental manipulation as central mechanic; making world changes visible and satisfying.
Adapted: terrain scars and recovery must be persistent and visible over long simulation runs.

NOT USED AS ART STYLE REFERENCES:
RimWorld, Townscaper, Bad North, Terra Nil, Fata Deum — useful for specific mechanics but not for visual direction.

TINY GODS VISUAL NORTH STAR:
A living mythographic atlas: part illuminated manuscript, part archaeological map, part strange ecological simulation, part dark myth.
Not generic dark-mode SaaS. Not parchment cosplay. Not direct imitation of any comparison game.

Key decisions derived from recon:
- Terrain needs silhouette distinction (REUS 2 principle)
- Settlements need progressive building density (SONGS OF SYX principle)
- Divine powers need anticipation/impact/aftermath sequence (UNIVERSIM principle)
- Creatures need readable shapes at small scale (DWARF FORTRESS principle)
- Weather must visibly alter mood (AGAINST THE STORM principle)
- World history must leave visible scars (WORLD BOX + FROM DUST principle)
